5. Tajweed — Original Study Lessons

These are original introductory explanations covering core Tajweed subjects. They are not a word-for-word copy of هدايا المستفيد في أحكام التجويد. Use the book itself with a teacher for its full treatment.

Lesson 1: Meaning and purpose

Tajweed is giving every letter its proper articulation point and characteristics during Qur’an recitation. Learn by listening and receiving correction from a qualified teacher.

Lesson 2: Makharij al-huruf

Letters emerge from specific areas including the throat, tongue, lips, nasal passage and open mouth/throat space. Practise each sound carefully.

Lesson 3: Noon sākinah and tanween

The four main rules are Iẓhār (clear pronunciation), Idghām (merging), Iqlāb (conversion) and Ikhfā’ (concealment). The following letter determines the rule.

Lesson 4: Meem sākinah

The principal rules are Ikhfā’ Shafawī before ب, Idghām Shafawī before م, and Iẓhār Shafawī with other letters.

Lesson 5: Madd

Madd is the lengthening of a vowel in specified circumstances. Its duration depends on its category and the recitation method being learned.

Lesson 6: Qalqalah

Qalqalah is a controlled echoing quality on ق ط ب ج د when they carry sukūn. Do not add an extra vowel sound.

Lesson 7: Tafkhīm and tarqīq

Tafkhīm is a fuller, heavier sound and tarqīq is a lighter sound. Rā’ and the word Allah have context-dependent rules.

Lesson 8: Waqf and ibtidā’

Waqf means stopping and ibtidā’ means beginning again. Observe the signs in your Mushaf and avoid stops that distort the meaning.

These summaries are introductory, not a substitute for a recognised Tajweed text and supervised recitation.
