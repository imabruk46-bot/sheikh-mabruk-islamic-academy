5. Introduction to Tajweed

Tajweed is the study and practice of reciting the Qur'an correctly, giving each letter its proper articulation and characteristics.

These are original introductory lessons covering important Tajweed topics. They are not a reproduction of the book هدايا المستفيد في أحكام التجويد.

Lesson 1: Meaning and Importance of Tajweed

Tajweed means giving each Arabic letter its correct point of articulation and qualities while reciting the Qur'an. Learning it helps a reader avoid pronunciation errors.

Lesson 2: Makhaarij al-Huroof (مَخَارِجُ الْحُرُوفِ)

Makhaarij refers to the places from which Arabic letters are pronounced. The main articulation areas are the empty space of the mouth and throat, the throat, the tongue, the lips and the nasal passage.

Lesson 3: Noon Saakinah and Tanween

Noon saakinah is a noon with no vowel. Tanween is the double vowel sign at the end of a word. Their well-known rules are Izhaar, Idghaam, Iqlaab and Ikhfaa.

Lesson 4: Meem Saakinah

The main rules of meem saakinah are Ikhfaa Shafawi, Idghaam Shafawi and Izhaar Shafawi. The correct application depends on the letter that follows the meem.

Lesson 5: Madd (الْمَدّ)

Madd means lengthening a sound. The basic madd letters are ا، و، ي when they meet their required conditions. Different types of madd have different recitation lengths.

Lesson 6: Qalqalah (الْقَلْقَلَة)

Qalqalah is a slight echoing sound when one of the letters ق ط ب ج د is sakin. The strength of the echo depends on the position and recitation context.

Lesson 7: Rules of Laam and Raa

The letter laam in the name of Allah and the letter raa have rules involving heavy and light pronunciation. These rules depend on their vowels and surrounding letters.

Lesson 8: Waqf and Ibtidaa

Waqf means stopping during recitation, while Ibtidaa means beginning again. A reader should learn where stopping is appropriate so the meaning is not distorted.

These lessons are introductory summaries, not a substitute for a complete Tajweed textbook or a qualified teacher's correction.
